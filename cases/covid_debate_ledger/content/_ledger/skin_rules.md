# Subject-specific writing rules (the "skin")

The universal rules — the bored reader test, paragraph architecture, no self-narration,
mechanism-over-apparatus, source discipline, voice, and the hard gates G0–G4 — live in the
shared `skill-ledger-write` assistant. **This file holds only the rules specific to THIS
subject.**

`skill-ledger-bootstrap` drafts a first version from your subject and your named reader; you

## This subject's rules — the recorded Rootclaim COVID-origins debate

The *case* is the **recorded debate itself** (Saar Wilf for lab-leak vs Peter Miller for
zoonosis, two judges, a $100,000 stake), not the primary virology. Every claim attributes a
*position held in the debate* to its voice, grounded in a verbatim quote, **without asserting the
position is true**. The job is to map who argued what, and how the judges weighed it — never to
re-adjudicate the origin question. Each rule below serves that neutrality.

- **D1 attribute, never assert** — every claim is "X said/argued/found Y", grounded in X's own
  verbatim words. Never let a debate position stand as the narrator's voice, and never state an
  origin conclusion the recorded voices did not. **[gate]**
- **D2 one ledger per voice** — atomise each source under its EXTRACTION.md row (`essay`,
  `adjudication`, `probabilistic-analysis`, `debate-transcript`) and route every claim to the
  ledger named for the voice that uttered it. Saar's turn is `wilf`, Peter's is `miller`, a judge's
  finding is `<judge>`, never merged. **[gate]**
- **D3 quote-pinned characterisation** — any claim that a statement is rhetorical, overstated, or
  "performed certainty" must pin to a verbatim span via an assessment record. A characterisation
  with no quoted span is an opinion, and opinions do not travel. **[gate]**
- **D4 verdict vs evidence** — keep the judges' *adjudicated outcome* (who won, and why they said
  they decided) distinct from the *evidence* the sides argued. The debate's result is a fact about
  the debate; it is not a fact about the virus.
- **D5 independence before agreement** — when two voices reach the same conclusion, look for a
  SHARED premise first (`Correlated-with`); two analyses leaning on the same market base-rate are
  not two independent updates. Let the derived double-count finding stand; do not hide it.
- **D6 method as its own axis** — the debate became a test of *reasoning method* (strict Bayesian
  aggregation vs ordinary expert judgement). Treat that meta-question as a distinct position, not as
  a proxy for which origin is correct.
