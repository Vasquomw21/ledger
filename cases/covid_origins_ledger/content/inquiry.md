# Inquiry — COVID-19 origins

## Q1 — Does the furin cleavage site indicate engineering?

**id:** fcs-engineering
**parent:** covid-origins

The spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 carries a polybasic (furin) cleavage site at the
S1/S2 boundary that is absent from its closest sampled relatives. Two readings of
the same observation are in print: that its novelty and apparent rarity point to
laboratory manipulation, and that such a site can arise by ordinary evolution and
its rarity reflects under-sampling rather than design.

**Sources by emphasis.** Natural origin: Andersen et al. (2020 #no-lab-scenario),
"The proximal origin of SARS-CoV-2" — argues the genomic features are consistent with
natural processes and that no laboratory scenario is plausible. Engineering-leaning:
Segreto & Deigin (2021 #lab-origin-not-excluded), "The genetic structure of SARS-CoV-2
does not rule out a laboratory origin" — argues the same features cannot exclude
manipulation.

**The crux.** Whether the FCS's apparent rarity is evidence of *design* or an
artefact of *under-sampling* the relevant viral diversity. Andersen's claim that a
polybasic cleavage site "can arise by a natural evolutionary process" is marked as
the crux of this sub-question: if it holds, rarity is not evidence of engineering.
Temmam et al. (2022 #no-fcs-in-relatives) sharpen the empirical state: the closest
sampled bat relatives (the BANAL viruses) still carry no furin cleavage site, so the
natural route is demonstrated in principle but unobserved in the nearest relatives —
a `qualifies` on Andersen's crux, not a refutation.

## Q2 — Does the early spatial and genomic evidence locate the origin at the market (zoonotic) rather than an introduced point source?

**id:** origin-locus
**parent:** covid-origins

A separate question from the FCS: *where and how* the outbreak began. Two strands
of early-2020 evidence are read as pointing to zoonotic emergence at the Huanan
market — the **spatial** epidemiology of the earliest cases (Worobey et al.
2022 #market-early-epicenter) and the **molecular** epidemiology of the earliest
genomes (Pekar et al. 2022 #multiple-introductions, two lineages → at least two
introductions). Temmam et al. (2022 #banal-infectious-humans) add that bat
relatives infectious for human cells via hACE2 already circulate, making natural
spillover mechanistically plausible.

**Sources by emphasis.** Zoonotic / market: Worobey et al.
(2022 #epicenter-spatial-test, spatial), Pekar et al. (2022 #two-lineages,
molecular). Natural-spillover plausibility: Temmam et al.
(2022 #banal-infectious-humans). The lab-origin side of *this* sub-question (an
introduced point source) is covered for the genome by Segreto & Deigin
(2021 #lab-origin-not-excluded); the primary lab-leak documentary evidence (e.g. the
DEFUSE proposal) is a declared Known gap.

**The shared-data caveat.** Both Worobey and Pekar rest on the same early-case
ascertainment around the market; their support for a zoonotic conclusion is
therefore **correlated**, not independent — recorded as a `Correlated-with` edge so
the derived double-count finding fires rather than counting them twice.
