# Finding — LHC collision safety

The public assurance states no mechanism: CERN's page says the collisions "would be perfectly
safe" and defers the basis to a linked report [rec: cern-perfectly-safe]. The technical safety
case has two branches — either any microscopic black hole evaporates by Hawking radiation, or,
if one were stable, it could not accrete dangerously. Under the stable-black-hole assumption the
conclusion rests on the astrophysical bound rather than on Hawking evaporation, and that bound is
marked the crux of the question [rec: astro-bound-crux]. Ledger exposes that conditional crux and
the recorded challenge and reply around it, without disputing the settled conclusion.

## The question

Two sub-questions ([[inquiry]]): could LHC collisions produce a dangerous stable microscopic black
hole (Q1), and what does the safety conclusion actually rest on (Q2)?

## What the machinery surfaces

The stable branch must remain safe if Hawking evaporation does not occur. Hawking evaporation
(LSAG 2008 #bh-hawking-decay) is stated in the review's abstract as an expectation, and the process
has never been observed. Giddings and Mangano separately analyse the stable case. In this graph that
branch runs through (Giddings & Mangano 2008 #astro-bound), whose quotation bounds accretion using
what white dwarfs and neutron stars are observed to survive.

That crux was contested rather than assumed. (Plaga 2008 #evades-exclusion) rebuts it, and the
rebuttal's aptness was adversarially reviewed and held [rec: plaga-rebuts-astro-apt-pass]; Giddings
and Mangano's published reply rebuts Plaga's scenario in turn, also reviewed and held
[rec: reply-rebuts-plaga-apt-pass]. Both directions are on the record.

Two things narrow the base of the stable branch. (Koch et al. 2009 #independent-argument) opens "we
independently present a short coherent argument", yet is declared correlated-with the crux
[rec: koch-giddings-correlated] — the mechanism differs, the evidence does not — so Ledger warns
that the two support edges into (LSAG 2008 #no-associated-risks) may not be independent. And the
step from the crux to (LSAG 2008 #stable-bh-no-danger) carries a sealed faithfulness dispute
[rec: astro-bound-faithfulness]: an observed astrophysical bound is restated as "no conceivable
danger".

The conditional conclusion is also stated more categorically: [rec: giddings-whatsoever] flags "no
risk of any significance whatsoever" in the same abstract as the conditional bound.

## What this does not show

This is not a safety argument against the LHC, and nothing here suggests the conclusion is wrong:
the risk-raising position was represented, challenged and answered in the peer-reviewed literature.
Nor does it show that Hawking evaporation has no role in the safety case — it is one branch of the
disjunction, and the graph traces the other, because a branch that must hold in the worst case is
the one that has to carry the argument alone. What is auditable is narrower: in this corpus the
stable branch runs through a single conditional bound, its second confirmation shares that bound's
evidence, and the public assurance names no mechanism at all. Correlation marks possible dependence
— it does not establish that two edges collapse into one. The machinery displays these judgements;
it does not settle them.
