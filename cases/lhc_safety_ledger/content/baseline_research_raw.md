**Question: Is the LHC safe from creating a dangerous black hole, and what is that conclusion based on?**

The scientific consensus is clear: the LHC is safe, and the production of dangerous black holes has
been ruled out. The conclusion rests on two main pillars.

**1. Hawking radiation.** Scientists almost universally agree that black holes evaporate, emitting
Hawking radiation. A microscopic black hole produced in a collision would therefore be extremely
unstable, decaying in the tiniest fraction of a second — long before it could interact with
surrounding matter. Any microscopic black holes produced at the LHC are expected to decay by Hawking
radiation before they even reach the detector walls.

**2. The cosmic-ray (astrophysical) argument.** Nature has been running this experiment for billions
of years. High-energy cosmic rays with energies far exceeding those of LHC collisions have been
striking the Earth, the Sun, and far denser bodies — white dwarfs and neutron stars — throughout
the history of the universe. If such collisions could produce dangerous, stable black holes, they
would already have destroyed these objects. Giddings and Mangano analysed this in detail: they noted
that a stable black hole produced by a symmetric LHC collision could be trapped by the Earth and
accrete slowly, whereas a cosmic-ray-produced one would be moving too fast to be captured — but they
closed this loophole by showing that black holes produced by cosmic rays hitting white dwarfs and
neutron stars would be stopped inside those dense bodies, and would have catalysed their collapse on
timescales far shorter than the observed lifetimes of such stars. The continued existence of old
white dwarfs and neutron stars is therefore direct empirical evidence that the LHC is safe.

**Consensus.** The safety conclusion has been endorsed by the CERN LHC Safety Assessment Group
(LSAG), reviewed and confirmed by CERN's Scientific Policy Committee, and endorsed by professional
physics societies; numerous world-renowned physicists, including Nobel laureates, have stated that
LHC collisions pose no danger. Legal challenges seeking to halt the LHC were dismissed.

**Bottom line.** There is no credible risk. Either the micro black holes evaporate immediately
(Hawking radiation), or — even if some theory of stable black holes held — the survival of white
dwarfs and neutron stars under eons of cosmic-ray bombardment shows they cannot grow dangerously.
The collider is safe to operate.
